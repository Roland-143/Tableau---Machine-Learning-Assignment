# Member 3 Presentation: Regional and Pandemic Analysis

## Slide 1 — Uneven Regional Growth

**Recommended chart:** Selected-state regional GDP trend lines, 2016–2025.

- Every selected-state regional total increased over the decade.
- South: +37.81%; West: +33.72% from 2016 to 2025.
- Northeast (+17.19%) and Midwest (+16.05%) had smaller cumulative increases.
- These are four-state sample aggregates, not complete Census-region totals.

**Speaker notes:**  
This chart compares the combined Real GDP of the four sampled states in each Census region, not all states in a region. All four lines rise between 2016 and 2025, but the South has the largest percentage increase: 37.81 percent. The West is next at 33.72 percent. The Northeast and Midwest increase by 17.19 and 16.05 percent. The trend also shows why we need to distinguish economic size from percentage growth: the regional totals start at different levels, so a larger line does not necessarily mean faster growth. Our data describes association and observed changes; it does not identify the causes of different regional trajectories.

## Slide 2 — The Shock Was Uneven; Rebounds Varied

**Recommended charts:** Side-by-side state growth bars for 2019–2020 and 2020–2021, with identical scales and regional colors.

- Illinois had the steepest 2020 decline (−4.96%); Arizona grew 1.81%.
- Florida led 2021 growth (+9.37%); Pennsylvania had the weakest rebound (+3.57%).
- The hardest-hit state did not have the strongest rebound.

**Speaker notes:**  
The left chart ranks state-level growth between 2019 and 2020. Fifteen states contracted. Illinois fell 4.96 percent, the largest decline, while Arizona was the exception and grew 1.81 percent. In the adjacent chart, every state grew in 2021, but the ranking changed: Florida led at 9.37 percent, whereas Pennsylvania grew 3.57 percent. Illinois rebounded by 6.61 percent, well below Florida. Pennsylvania’s 2021 level was still about 0.68 percent below its 2019 level. So a positive rebound rate is not the same as a full recovery to the earlier level, and the data does not support a simple rule that the steepest decline produced the fastest rebound.

## Slide 3 — Recovery Looked Different by State and Region

**Recommended chart:** Region-then-state × year heat map colored by state YoY growth; optional regional growth lines.

- The Midwest had the deepest regional 2020 decline (−3.60%); the West had the mildest (−0.84%).
- South-region selected-state growth averaged 4.23% in 2022–2025 versus 3.21% in 2017–2019; West growth slowed from 4.43% to 2.38%.
- Takeaway: disruption and recovery were heterogeneous, not a single uniform path.

**Speaker notes:**  
The heat map puts the 2020 contraction and 2021 increase into the context of all ten years and shows which states differed from their regional pattern. At the regional aggregate level, the Midwest contracted 3.60 percent in 2020, the largest regional decline; the West fell 0.84 percent, the smallest. After 2021, the South’s mean annual growth in 2022 through 2025 was 4.23 percent, above its 3.21 percent average in 2017 through 2019. The West shifted in the opposite direction, from 4.43 to 2.38 percent. These sample-based summaries reinforce that recovery was uneven. They are descriptive and should not be generalized to every state or interpreted as causal effects.
